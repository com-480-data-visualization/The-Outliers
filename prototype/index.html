Chapter 1

The Explosion

For decades, orbital space was a slow frontier. Then everything changed. 75.5% of all operational satellites were launched from 2019 onward.

In 1974, only a handful of operational satellites circled Earth. Space was reserved for superpowers.

Through the 1990s, growth was steady. The total crossed 400 satellites by 1998 -mostly government and military.

The 2010s brought CubeSats and commercial actors. By 2018, over 2,000 satellites were in orbit.

Then came the mega-constellations. In 2021 alone, ~1,450 satellites were launched -more than in the entire 1990s.

By January 2023, 6,713 operational satellites crowd the space around Earth. The curve is not slowing down.

What are those satellites for?

The cumulative count split by purpose. Communications was a modest band through 2018, then absorbs nearly every new launch from 2019 onward as Starlink and OneWeb deploy their fleets. By 2022, 72% of every active satellite is for communications.

    Chapter 4

    The Next Decade

    Today's 6,713 satellites are not the ceiling. They are the floor. Public filings with the FCC, the ITU and national regulators already cover more than 80,000 satellites for deployment by ~2030: a 12-fold expansion of the orbital population in roughly seven years.

    Today vs. tomorrow

    Counts represent satellites filed with regulators (FCC, ITU, national agencies) for deployment by approximately 2030. Actual deployments depend on launch cadence, regulatory approvals and constellation viability, so the upper bound may slip — but the trajectory does not.
